Before you drop the hook

Four things that turn a good day into an expensive one.

Anchor on sand, never on Posidonia

The dark meadows under you are protected seagrass. Balearic law (Decree 25/2018) requires you to drop on a clear sand patch — look for the pale ground, or check a Posidonia layer before you let go. Fines are real and start in the thousands.

Cabrera needs a permit

The national park allows a fixed number of mooring buoys per night and they are booked online, in advance, through the Balearic government. Turning up without one is not a plan.

Three knots inside the buoys

Keep to 3 knots within 300 m of any beach, stay outside the yellow swim-zone buoys, and land people by dinghy or by swimming — not by nosing the boat onto the sand.

The swell outlasts the wind

A Tramuntana that blew itself out overnight still leaves a northerly swell rolling into the Serra coves the next morning. Check the wave forecast, not just the wind arrow.

How the scoring works: each anchorage is stored with the compass direction it opens onto. The app compares that to the wind you set, adds the fetch that coast is exposed to, and converts the result into an effective sea state. Distances follow the coastline rather than a straight line, so rounding a cape counts. Times assume the cruising speed of the boat you picked plus time at anchor.

Planning aid only — not a substitute for a chart, a forecast, or your own judgement. Check AEMET or Windy and the local port authority before you leave, and remember that headlands (Formentor, Cap Blanc, Cap de ses Salines) accelerate the wind well above the number on the slider.
